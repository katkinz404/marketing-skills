## The consultant's own North Carolina setup (not legal or tax advice)

| Item | What the source says | Source | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales tax on consulting | Charges for professional services such as consulting or research that result only in a report are not part of a taxable sale; advertising-agency charges tied to tangible items are | 17 N.C. Admin. Code 07B .0901, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/north-carolina/17-N-C-Admin-Code-07B-0901 (government, not read at source) | Separately state consulting on its own invoice line |
| Sales tax on digital products | Certain digital property, including a report or publication delivered electronically, is taxable at the state rate plus local | NCDOR, Certain Digital Property, https://www.ncdor.gov/taxes-forms/sales-and-use-tax/certain-digital-property (government, not read at source) | Sell no downloadable template pack or written report as a product until a CPA answers with the exact description in hand |
| Trade name | A sole proprietor trading under any name other than her own files an assumed business name certificate with the register of deeds in the county of the business office; up to five names on one certificate | NC Secretary of State, Assumed Business Names (Article 14A, effective 2017-12-01), https://www.sosnc.gov/divisions/business_registration/assumed_business_names (government, not read at source) | File before invoice one if a trade name is used; the payee name on the check must match |

## Questions for the consultant's own CPA and attorney

**CPA**
1. Is a fixed-fee consulting engagement delivered as a print-ready file subject to North Carolina sales tax as a service, or as certain digital property? Does the answer change if a printed pad is delivered?
2. Should invoices separately state consulting and any printed or digital item, and how should a tax line read until the answer is settled?
3. As a first-year sole proprietor, how are checks received at signing recognized for estimated taxes, and when must an entity be formed, if ever?
4. What must be handed to a client in advance (W-9) if he may pay $2,000 or more in a year, and what is the current 1099-NEC threshold?
5. Is a mileage log at the standard rate the right treatment for a four-county circuit?

**Attorney (or the free SBDC or SCORE review)**
1. Review the one-page engagement letter: the refund clock, the acceptance criteria, the liability cap at fees paid, the no-consequential-losses line, the termination line.
2. Does supplying a printed quote-sheet layout with labeled blanks for cancellation, warranty, and insurance text stay outside the practice of law, and what scope language protects the consultant if a client's form later fails?
3. If a trade name is used, confirm the assumed business name filing and the payee line.
