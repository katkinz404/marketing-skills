# Numbers Not to Use

Figures that circulate in this market and must not appear on any page, sheet, or pitch. Each one is either unverified, misattributed, old, or a vendor's self-interested claim. Where a safe substitute exists it is named.

| Number | Where it comes from | Why not | Say instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| "A complete Google profile gets 7x more clicks" | Vendor blogs attributing it to Google; no Google page carrying it was found (retrieved 2026-09-15) | Unverifiable attribution | Google's own guidance: complete and accurate profiles are more likely to show in local results |
| "Profiles with photos get 42% more direction requests and 35% more website clicks" | Vendor pages attributing it to Google; not found on a Google page | Same | Nothing; do not quantify photos |
| "53% of mobile users leave after 3 seconds" | Google publisher research from about 2016 to 2017, republished by vendors | Old, about publisher sites, not trade pages | Test the page on a phone on a cellular connection |
| "A word-of-mouth impression is worth 5 to 100 times a paid one" | WOMMA figure cited by SCORE, undated | Vague and undated | Referrals need a plan, an ask, and a mechanism |
| "254% average client growth" | One contractor-marketing vendor's page, no method | No method | Nothing |
| "Painters close 22% of bids; the top quartile closes 45 to 51%; 38% of lost bids stall in follow-up" | One vendor blog citing "PCA 2025 benchmarks"; no PCA page found | Single unverified secondary source | Ask him: how many quotes went out, how many said yes |
| "28% of inbound leads convert at a 42-minute response; 62% under two minutes" | ServiceTitan report figures seen only second-hand | Not read at source, not painting-specific | Ask him who answers when he is on a ladder |
| "Responding within 5 minutes makes you 21 times more likely to qualify a lead" | MIT and InsideSales lead-response study, 2007; often misattributed to HBR 2011 | Old, B2B web-lead data | Internal use only; never on his paper |
| "Contact within an hour is 7 times more likely to qualify" | Harvard Business Review, March 2011, 2,241 firms | Old, B2B web leads | Internal use only |
| "3 or 4 new reviews will get you to 4.5 stars" | Repeated across drafts in this project | Arithmetic says 12 for a 4-review 3.0 | The formula: (12 + 5n) divided by (4 + n) reaches 4.5 at n = 12 |
| "Tier 1 should be 50 to 60% of tier 2" | Coaching-site content on productized pricing, no study | No evidence | Price against a tenth of one of his jobs |
| "$131,000 average revenue per painting business" | Two IBISWorld national totals divided by each other (judgment) | A national mean, not a local median | Ask him what a typical job runs and how many he does |
| "E-signature raises close rates 15% and closes 60% faster" | Proposify State of Proposals 2026, vendor data from its own users | Self-reported B2B software data | "One national vendor's figure," context only, never his forecast |
| "Follow-up lifts close rates from 12 to 18% to 30 to 35%" | JobEstimator and Level CFO blogs, no method | Vendor, low confidence | A hypothesis his own log will confirm or kill |
| "Online payers get paid 4 times faster" | Jobber report, vendor | Self-interested | Ask him how many days until the final check clears |
| Any "more jobs" or "double your close rate" line | Nowhere; do not write one | Federal enforcement in 2025 and 2026 targeted growth and earnings claims made to small businesses (government, retrieved 2026-09-15, not read at source) | The straight-talk line |

## Rule

If a number is not his, it does not go on his paper. If it goes in a reference file, it carries a source, a date, a tier, and "not read at source" until someone opens the page.
