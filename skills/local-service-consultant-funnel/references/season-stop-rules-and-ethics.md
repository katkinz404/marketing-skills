# Season, Stop Rules, and Ethics

Three parts: the season calendar that decides when a trade can be pitched, the stop and cash rules that keep building from eating selling, and the ethics and legal boundaries in plain words. ### What it means for outreach

- Exterior painters, stain, and pressure washing: a favor card and a tailgate pitch on rain days only during the prime weeks, never on a job site; a real pitch in the first weeks after the season closes; nothing in late November and December (deer season, holidays).
- Interior and year-round trades (handymen, cleaners, small remodelers, flooring): pitchable on the slow week they name, any month. "Slow" is a hypothesis per trade until he says it; do not assume a trade is slow because a category sounds like it should be.
- The screen at touch one is "when does it go quiet for you?" Any owner who names a week inside the window goes on the list. Anyone who says spring is a March file.

## Part 2: stop rules and cash rules

| Rule | Reason |
|---|---|
| 10 conversations and zero sheets seen: change the ask, not the product | The ask is wrong, not the page |
| 5 offers on paper and zero signed: change the trade or the scope, not the price | Price is under a tenth of a job already; the fit is the problem |
| Any week building hours beat selling hours after selling starts: cut the build | Building is the biggest threat to the date (two of five designs specified 40+ build hours before a conversation) |
| Deadline minus 30 days with no offer on paper: stop all building | Nothing built now can be sold in time |
| Fewer than 15 names with a venue by the end of week one: decide on radius or remote that day | Denominator problem; a 1-in-4 sheet rate on 12 names is three sheets |
| Cash out before the first signature over one sale's worth: stop spending | The pre-signature ceiling |
| Any monthly software not live and earning at deadline minus 30: keep-or-pause decision | It bills whether or not a page exists |
| Line six of the five-decision sheet checked every Friday against the signed column | The month the practice must pay the consultant is the real deadline |
| Pre-sale build capped at three artifacts (one-pager, one-page letter, target list with venues) until a real blank sheet is in hand | Every other template is filled from the first real sheet, not from a guess |

## Part 3: ethics and legal boundaries

### The never list

- Never work from a document the prospect did not hand you. A customer's copy of his quote, a relative's copy, a counter clerk's copy: out of every audit, pitch, template, example, and conversation. The reason: it is private customer paper, and using it is the one mistake that would be discovered in a small county and end the relationship.
- Never hold a login. Delegated access on the client's own account only.
- Never send anything in a client's name without a standing written authorization and a per-message yes.
- Never text a prospect who has not texted you first.
- Never cite a statute, a misdemeanor, a tax rule, an industry standard number, or a competitor's name to a tradesman. It reads as a threat, and the consultant is not his lawyer. The commercial gap (no total, no start window, no place to sign) is enough.
- Never comment on a cash price, and never hint it is a tax matter.
- Never write a growth or earnings claim.
- Never invent proof.
- Never publish anything without the client's written permission at the level he initialed.
