# Homeowner Script Card

The two sentences a tradesman says when he hands a long-time customer a sheet that now has a deposit line and a place to sign. Printed on the back of the customer's copy. It exists because the person on the other side of the sheet reads a deposit line as a change in the relationship, and nobody had written what to say (panel finding, judgment).

## The script

"I've started putting everything on paper so nobody has to remember what we agreed. Half when we start, the rest when you've walked it with me and you're happy."

Swap "half" for whatever his deposit line says, in his words.

## Three variations

**Long-time handshake customer.** "You and I have never needed paper, and we still don't for trust. I'm doing this for everyone now so my numbers are in one place. Same deal as always, just written down."

**New customer.** "Everything we talked about is on this one page: what I'm doing, what it costs, when I start, and when you pay. You sign it, I sign it, and neither of us has to remember."

**Absent owner, approving by text and photo.** "You'll get a photo before I start and a photo when I'm done. If I find something I didn't quote, I send a picture and a price, and nothing changes unless you text back yes."

## Test protocol before printing fifty

1. Say the script on one real quote with the consultant present. Write down what the customer said back, word for word.
2. Print a 10-sheet trial pad. Use it for a week.
3. Change any word the customer stumbled on. Then print fifty.

## Rules

- The script is his, in his words. The consultant drafts; he edits; he says it.
- No line about trust being broken, money trouble, or "my accountant told me." The reason is "so nobody has to remember."
- If a customer refuses the deposit line, that is data for the log, not a reason to remove the line the next day.
