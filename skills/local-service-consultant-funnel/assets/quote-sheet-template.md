# Quote Sheet Template

The rung 1A product: the tradesman's quote sheet, rebuilt in his name, on a two-part carbonless pad. Every line is one he already fills in his head, can circle, or can leave off. Nothing he would leave blank stays on the sheet. The consultant writes no legal, tax, or warranty text; those are labeled blanks for his own advisers.

## Section 1: the sheet, as printed

```
[HIS BUSINESS NAME]                                         No. ______
[Owner name]  [phone]  [town, state]
Checks payable to [his business name]                       Date: ________

Customer: ______________________   Phone: ______________   OK to text?  Y / N
Job address: _________________________________________________________

What the job covers
Area or surface            What will be done                 Product or material
______________________     ______________________________    ________________
______________________     ______________________________    ________________
______________________     ______________________________    ________________
Not included: ____________________________________________________________

Total                                                        $ ____________
Cash or check price, and what it covers                      $ ____________
Labor price held until  ____ / ____ / ____
Materials: [circle one]  included in the total  /  billed at receipt

DEPOSIT AT SIGNING (covers materials)                        $ ____________
BALANCE DUE AT THE WALKTHROUGH, touch-ups listed and initialed below
   Touch-ups: _____________________________________  Initials: ______

Start window: ______________________   Weather note, in his words: ______________
Payment accepted: [circle]  cash  /  check  /  card  /  [other]

Accepted by: ______________________________   Date: ____ / ____ / ____

[Cancellation notice, if any, supplied by your attorney]
```

The two lines in capitals sit near the top of the money block on purpose. The deposit at signing is the only line that enforces itself: no check, no materials, no start. The balance at the walkthrough is where most final checks get lost.

## Section 2: the back of the sheet

The homeowner script, two sentences, printed on the customer's copy. Text and variations in assets/homeowner-script-card.md.

## Section 3: pad print spec

| Item | Spec |
|---|---|
| Form | Two-part carbonless (customer copy on top, his copy underneath) |
| Numbering | Pre-numbered, starting where his old pad left off if he wants continuity |
| Size | Match his current pad so it fits the same clipboard |
| First run | A 10-sheet trial pad, used on one real quote with the script, before any larger run |
| Second run | 50 sheets after the trial [judgment cost, get a quote from the printer] |
| Who pays the printer | The client, directly; the consultant sends the file and the printer bills him |
| Proof | The client approves the printer's proof himself |

## Section 4: rebuild rules

1. The template is filled from the first real blank sheet he hands over, not from this file. Copy his line order, his words for the trade, his numbering.
2. Ask before adding any line: "Do you fill this in today, in your head or on paper?" No means it comes off.
3. Product, coats, prep level, and warranty are his to write in the scope lines if he chooses. They are not pre-printed.
4. Keep cash and check first on the payment line. Add card only if he takes cards.
5. The consultant never writes the cancellation text, a warranty, a tax line, or any wording about the cash price. The labeled blank stays until his attorney or CPA fills it.
6. Deliver the print-ready file and a texted copy. Delivered means the file is in his hands and one real quote has been written together, not that the printer has finished.
