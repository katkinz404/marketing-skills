# Receipt and Invoice

Two templates. The receipt is torn from a numbered book at the table when a check or cash changes hands. The invoice states every line separately so a CPA can treat each line on its own.

## Numbered receipt (at the table)

```
[BUSINESS NAME]                                   Receipt No. ______
[Town, state]  [phone]                            Date: ____ / ____ / ____

Received from: ______________________________
Amount: $ __________
For: [Quote Sheet Rebuild / Phone and Reviews Afternoon / Season Tune-Up]
Paid by: [circle]  check no. ________  /  cash  /  payment link
Received by: ______________________________
```

Two-part receipt book: he keeps the top copy. The consultant's copy is the cash record.

## Invoice (separately stated lines)

```
[BUSINESS NAME]                                   Invoice No. ______
[Town, state]  [phone]  [email]                   Date: ____ / ____ / ____
Bill to: [client business name], [town]

Line 1  Consulting: [rung name], delivered [date]           $ __________
Line 2  Printed or digital items, if any: [describe]         $ __________
        Sales tax on line 2, if any:                         [per CPA]
Total                                                        $ __________

Paid in full at signing on [date] by [check no. / cash / link]. Thank you.
Checks payable to [business name].
```

## Rules

- Consulting goes on its own line, always. Anything printed or delivered as a file goes on a separate line. Whether either line carries sales tax is the CPA's answer; until then the line reads [per CPA].
- The printer bills the client directly for pads. The consultant does not resell printing.
- Hand a W-9 in advance to any client who may pay $2,000 or more in a year, so a man with no email is not caught out in January (CPA lens, judgment; preparer to confirm the current threshold).
- Number receipts and invoices in one sequence per year. Never reuse a number.
- Keep a mileage log next to the receipt book; the two are read together at tax time.
