# Referrer Log

Referrals do not arrive on their own. They are asked for, earned first, and closed with a report-back. This log tracks the five connectors and everyone who sends a name after them.

## The three lines

**The reciprocity offer, first:** "Before I ask you anything, is there one thing I can fix for you? Your listing says one phone number and your sign says another. I'll write up which is right and hand it to you."

**The ask, second:** "Who writes quotes on a pad and is slow right now? I'll leave three of these. There's a line on the back for your name so he knows who sent him."

**The report-back, within 14 days of any name:** "You gave me [first name]'s name. Here's what happened. He [took the card, said not now, signed]. Thank you either way."

## Why this is the mechanism

No published evidence shows that paint counters, lumber counters, or chambers refer marketing consultants (research gap, 2026-09-15). What turns a one-time name into a stream, by the judgment of people who have done it, is two things: doing something for the referrer before asking, and closing the loop on every name within two weeks. SCORE's referral guidance says the same in general terms: referrals need a plan, a direct ask, and a mechanism, and the worst time to ask is at the moment of payment (industry body, retrieved 2026-09-15, not read at source).

## Rules

- Never leave a one-pager without filling in the "sent by" line first.
- Never ask a referrer for a name in the same breath as delivering bad news about one.
- Report back even when nothing happened. "He said not now" keeps the referrer warm; silence ends it.
- A referrer who sends two names in a season gets the next favor before anyone else.
