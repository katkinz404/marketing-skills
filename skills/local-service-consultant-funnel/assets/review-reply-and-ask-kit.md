# Review Reply and Ask Kit

Rung 1B: the Phone and Reviews Afternoon. The consultant drafts; the client posts every reply himself from his own login. One neutral ask goes to every finished customer. No reward, no filter, no scripted wording for the customer.

## Reply drafts (he edits, he posts)

**Positive review.** "Thank you, [first name]. The [deck, porch, kitchen] came out the way we talked about, and I'm glad it shows. Call me when the next one comes up."

**Negative review about the work.** "Thank you for telling me. I'd rather hear it than not. I want to see it myself and make it right. Please call me at [phone] and I'll come out this week."

**Negative review about a helper, driver, or truck.** "I'm sorry about that, and I've taken it up with the person involved. That's not how I want anyone treated on my account. If you'd like to talk it through, call me at [phone]."

**Off-topic or unclear.** "Thanks for the note. I'm not sure this was meant for me, but if there's something I can help with, my number is [phone]."

Rules for every reply: under 60 words; thank them; one specific line; invite a call; never argue a fact without evidence in hand; never mention another customer.

## The neutral ask card (client-facing)

Sent to every finished customer, in the same words, one to three days after final payment.

"Thank you for having me out. If you have a minute, a review helps a one-truck shop more than anything: [link]. Good or bad, I read every one."

Same words for every customer. No gift, no discount, no "if you were happy." No asking only the customers who seemed pleased.

## Timing and habit

- The ask goes out one to three days after the final check clears, from his phone, in his name.
- Ten minutes every week: read new reviews, post the drafted reply, log the count.
- The consultant never posts from his account and never holds his login. If she helps on a profile, he adds her as a manager and keeps ownership.

## The arithmetic, said plainly

A profile with 4 reviews at 3.0 needs 12 new five-star reviews to reach a true 4.5. The formula is (12 + 5n) divided by (4 + n), which reaches 4.5 at n = 12. Three or four new reviews do not get there. About two in three people who are asked leave a review (67%, BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, research firm, retrieved 2026-09-15, not read at source), so plan on about 18 asks. Say the arithmetic; never say the study.

## Missed-call text-back (set up with him typing)

On a free tier of whatever his phone or a free tool offers, a text that fires when he cannot answer: "This is [first name] at [business]. I'm on a ladder. Text me the address and what you need and I'll call you back by [time]."

He types it. He owns the account. The consultant sits next to him.

## Boundaries (internal note, never recited to him)

- The FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465, effective 2024-10-21, government) bans incentives conditioned on a review's sentiment and fake reviews. Retrieved 2026-09-15, not read at source.
- Google's Maps content policy bans review gating (asking only happy customers) and incentives for reviews (vendor policy, retrieved 2026-09-15, not read at source).
- The kit is built so both are impossible to break by accident: one identical ask, to everyone, with nothing attached.
