# One-Pager Leave-Behind

The one piece of paper a tradesman takes home. Printed on card stock. Front and back. It carries one price and no results claim. Words follow the table in SKILL.md: quote, the pad, deposit, final check, walkthrough, silent quote; never the words on the never list.

## Front

**[Business name]**
[Town], [state]. Call or text [phone].

**One page between your quote and your last check.**

I fix what happens between the handshake and the last check. Quotes that go quiet. Checks that come late. Reviews nobody answered. The phone when you're on a ladder.

**What you hold when we're done**

- Your quote sheet, rebuilt in your name: what the job covers, the total, when you start, a deposit when they sign, the balance at the walkthrough, and a place to sign.
- Two sentences for your customer, printed on the back of the pad.
- A card for the truck: what to send when a quote goes quiet.
- A one-line log so you can see which quotes came back.
- One real quote, written together.

**[First price, default $300], once.** You own it. No monthly. No contract. I never ask for a password.

**How it works**

1. Hand me a blank quote sheet, or text me a photo of one.
2. We talk for 20 minutes, at your tailgate or on the phone.
3. You get the page in [turnaround] business days.

"I'm new at this as a business. I don't have another contractor's results to show you. I'll show you your own numbers first." [First name]

## Back

Two questions I'll ask you:

1. What's the slowest part of getting paid?
2. When does it go quiet for you?

Sent by: ______________________

[Business name]. [Phone]. [Email].
I am not a lawyer, CPA, or insurance adviser. Nothing here is a guarantee.

## Print spec

| Item | Spec |
|---|---|
| Size | Letter, or half-letter if the copy fits at 12 point |
| Stock | Card stock, matte, so it survives a truck |
| Quantity | 50 for the first run [judgment cost $20 to $40, check the printer] |
| Color | One color plus black is enough; the price line in the second color |
| Do not print | Any number above the first price; statistics; testimonials; star counts; a countdown; a fictional example |

## Rules for this piece

- One price. The rest of the ladder is spoken, later, after he has paid once.
- The straight-talk line stays. A few buyers will use "new" to ask for $200. That is a buying signal, not an insult.
- The "sent by" line is what turns a referrer's name into a stream: fill it in before you hand three of these to a connector.
- Reprint only after the first real quote sheet has taught you what to change.
