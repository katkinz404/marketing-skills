# Proof Permission Form

One page, attached to the engagement letter as a separate sheet. Opt-in. Never a condition of the work. Never paid for. He can decline and the work is unchanged.

## The form

**May I describe this work to other tradespeople?**

Initial one line, or none.

____ **A. Layout only.** You may show a blank copy of the before and after paperwork, with my business name, my numbers, and every customer covered.

____ **B. First name, trade, and town.** You may say "[first name], a [trade] in [town]" and use the counts from my quote log (quotes sent, signed, deposits taken, balances paid at the walkthrough). No dollar amounts. No customer names.

____ **C. Full write-up.** You may use my business name and the counts above, and a sentence in my own words that I approve first.

____ **None.** Nothing about this work is described to anyone.

I can change this in writing at any time, and anything already published comes down within 14 days of my note.

Nothing was given to me in exchange for this permission.

Signed: ______________________ Date: ________ Business: ______________________

## Rules for the consultant

- Ask at the day 20 or 30 check-in, after the log has been read together, never at the moment of payment.
- Record the answer, yes or no, in the results ledger. A "none" is a complete answer.
- If anything is ever given in exchange for a write-up (a discount, a free tune-up), the write-up carries a plain disclosure line: "[First name] received [what] in connection with this work." The Federal Trade Commission's Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, government, from memory, not read at source) are the reason. Simpler: give nothing in exchange.
- Never publish a quote-log ratio (signed divided by sent) under a client's name in a small county. Level B allows counts, not ratios, for that reason.
- Keep the signed copy with the engagement letter. Log the level and date in assets/tally-sheet.md notes.
