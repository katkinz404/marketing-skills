# Quote Check Pocket Read

The free version of the Quote Check. Forty-five minutes, public facts only, six rows. It stays in your pocket until he asks. The only thing that leaves your pocket is one index card with one fact and one thing to do.

## Ethics box

Read this before the first search, every time.

- No timed calls. No calls at all for the read. Phone answering is scored from what he tells you: "who answers when you're on a ladder?"
- No mystery shopping. No fake quote request. No asking a friend to call him.
- No recording of anything.
- No personas. If you contact him, it is under your own name, for your own reason.
- Nothing from a document he did not hand over. A quote sheet a customer, a relative, or a counter clerk shows you is out. Row 6 stays blank until his own hand gives you a blank one.
- Nothing you learn here is repeated to anyone but him.

## Time cap

Forty-five minutes from the first search to the finished card. Set a timer. When it rings, stop, even in the middle of a row. Write "not checked" in any empty row. A full local review runs four hours or more (Semrush and StoreRocket guides, vendor, retrieved 2026-09-15, not read at source); that is paid work, not a favor.

## What goes on the favor card

One index card. One fact. One thing to do. Framed as your own experience, with the date you saw it.

| Side | Content |
|---|---|
| Front, line 1 | The fact, in the words from the table above, with the date |
| Front, line 2 | The one thing to do about it, something he can do tonight for free |
| Front, line 3 | [your first name], [town]. [your phone] |
| Back | Blank |

Rules for choosing the row:

- Pick the row where the fix is free and takes him under an hour.
- Never row 6. Never two facts on one card.
- Never a number from a source. Only what you saw, with the date you saw it.
- Never the words poor, weak, bad, losing, or unprofessional. Never "you're losing jobs."
- If every row is clean, the card says what is working, one line, and nothing to do. That is still a favor.

## Worked example, generic

A painting contractor in a county of about 12,000 people. Google shows 3.0 stars on 4 reviews. Both negatives are about a driver, not the paint, and both are unanswered. Phone only for quotes. Name matches everywhere; phone differs on one site. Last post four months ago. No sheet seen.

Card, front: "I read your reviews on [date]. The two that complain have nothing under them from you, so the next person reading them hears one side of the story. A two-line answer under each, tonight, changes that. [first name], [town]. [phone]"

Row 2 wins because the fix is free, takes ten minutes, and is his to do. Row 4 is second. Row 6 waits for a blank sheet.
