# Local-Service Consultant Funnel

Sell one page a tradesman can hold, from someone who has already been useful, at a price under a tenth of one of his jobs, never monthly on a first purchase. Run the funnel in person first. Put one screen on the web when the business name is settled. Build the full web funnel when demand proves it is worth building. Write the SOP from what actually happened.

This skill is for the consultant's own business. It inherits every rule in local-service-sales-page and adds the ones below.

## Non-negotiable rules

- Label every statement: verified business fact, direct customer evidence, hypothesis to test, or unknown. Unknowns about the consultant stay in `[brackets]` and never publish.
- Never invent clients, results, testimonials, review counts, credentials, or outcomes. With no case study, the proof section of every asset is process clarity, owner visibility, and a published price.
- Every call to action names the real next state. "Request a free quote check" leads to a read, not a client relationship. "Sign and pay" leads to intake, not a finished deliverable. Nothing says "Book."
- Never work from a document the prospect did not hand you. A quote sheet a customer, relative, or third party gave you is out of every audit, pitch, template, and example.
- Never hold a login. Access is a delegated role on the consultant's own account, and the client keeps ownership. Say it on every sheet: "I never ask for a password."
- Never send anything in a client's voice without a standing written authorization and a per-message approval. Text a prospect only after he has texted you.
- No cold marketing texts. No cold email without a real postal address on it. No timed test calls, no personas, no mystery shopping, no recording.
- No earnings or growth claims, ever: no "more jobs," no "double your close rate," no number you cannot show him from his own log. Say the straight-talk line instead.
- Never cite a statute, a misdemeanor, a tax rule, an industry standard, or a competitor's name to a tradesman. Never comment on a cash price. Legal, tax, and insurance lines on his paperwork are labeled blanks for his own advisers.
- Words on every sheet: quote, the pad, deposit, final check, walkthrough, silent quote, one price paid once, you own it. Words never on a sheet: system, funnel, CRM, pipeline, onboarding, retainer, subscription, monthly, audit, founding client, leads guaranteed, ROI.
- Nothing publishes, no money is spent, and no message goes out without the consultant's explicit approval.

## Stage 0: Your own paperwork first

You are selling "get your paperwork right." Yours has to pass the same test before the first conversation.

1. Fill `assets/five-decision-sheet.md` in one sitting: the name on the invoice and the check, where the money lands, the price floor and the first price, the service radius, the reply window you can keep on a bad week, and line six: monthly fixed cost, months of runway, and the month the practice must pay you.
2. Apply the runway test. If the practice must pay you before the second sale can land, change the plan before anything else (raise the first price if the first three tailgate conversations do not flinch, sell 1A and 1B to the same client as two sales, or move the first tune-up earlier). If runway is under three months, stop and say so. A $300 plan is not the answer.
3. Fill `assets/consultant-truth-ledger.md`. On day one it has one verified fact: a written process exists. Everything else is a hold.
4. Set the pre-signature cash ceiling at one sale's worth. Inside it: card stock, index cards, a 10-sheet trial pad, fuel, one paid CPA question. Outside it until a signature exists: paid attorney review, insurance binding, filings. Use the free SBDC or SCORE review for the one-page letter now.
5. If you trade under any name but your own, file the assumed business name before invoice one. A sole proprietor invoicing under her legal name is clean; confirm with a CPA.
6. Separately state every invoice line: consulting on one line, anything digital or tangible on another. Sell no downloadable product until a CPA has answered with the exact product description in hand.
7. Start the mileage and hours log the same day.

## Stage 1: Positioning and the ideal customer

Internal category: evidence-led conversion systems for local service owners who quote in person. Nobody hears that sentence.

Porch line, the only positioning a buyer hears: "I fix what happens between the handshake and the last check. Quotes that go quiet, checks that come late, reviews nobody answered, and the phone when you're on a ladder. One price, paid once, no monthly, and you keep every login."

Straight-talk line, said early: "I'm new at this as a business. I don't have another contractor's results to show you. I'll show you your own numbers first."

A name goes on the target list only when all four are true:

1. Owner plus zero to two helpers, one truck, one phone, quotes at the property.
2. A visible trigger: an unanswered negative review, a name or phone mismatch across listings, no way to request a quote without calling, or a referrer's story of a late final check or a quote that went quiet.
3. A typical job of about $3,000 or more, so the first price stays under a tenth of one job. Ask "what does a typical job run?" before any price appears. Under $3,000, the offer is 1B, not 1A.
4. A slow week he names before your deadline, and a venue where you can meet him that is not a customer's job site.

Anti-profile, in writing: multi-crew shops with an office manager; anyone who wants a lead or appointment guarantee; anyone who wants you to log in, post in his voice, or get reviews with a reward; anyone who wants ads before the paper is fixed; anyone whose paperwork reached you through a third party.

## Stage 2: The ladder

Nothing monthly on a first purchase. Prices below are judgment pending the consultant's price floor; anchors are in `references/ladder-and-offer-cards.md`.

| Rung | Name on the sheet | What he holds | Price and terms |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | The favor | One index card: one public fact about his business and one thing to do about it. A verbal read of his blank quote sheet at the tailgate. No written report | Free, nothing asked |
| 1A | Quote Sheet Rebuild | A rebuilt quote sheet in his name: scope lines, total, start window, deposit at signing, balance at walkthrough, payment methods, acceptance line, a labeled blank for his attorney's cancellation text. The homeowner script on the back. A three-touch silent-quote card. A one-line quote log. A one-page five-row review. One real quote written together | $300, 100% at signing |
| 1B | Phone and Reviews Afternoon | Every existing review answered (you draft, he posts). The neutral review-ask card. A missed-call text-back on a free tier, set up with him typing. The one-page review. Fits any trade, needs no pad | $300, 100% at signing |
| 2 | Season Tune-Up | Rescored page, quote log read together, sheet and prices updated, profile refreshed with his photos and him logged in, review replies drafted, referral push | $500 per visit, at the visit, nothing renews |
| 3 | Quote-to-Check Package | Half A: a strategy page filled with his labeled numbers, payment and scheduling set up with him typing, follow-up and review kits, pad reprint spec, monthly log read. Half B: one quote-request page plus thank-you page built with local-service-sales-page | $850 A, $650 B, $1,500 both; 50% at signing, 50% at walkthrough; off-season only, to a rung 1 buyer who asks |

Pick 1A or 1B from row one of his review. Never show a menu. A lower spoken fallback exists only after five offers on paper and is never written down.

Exclusions printed on every rung: no monthly fee, no contract term, nothing renews, no lead guarantee, no password ever, nothing sent in his name, no legal, tax, or insurance advice, no comment on any cash price, no ads, no review rewards or filters, no work from a document that did not come from him.

Refund on rung 1: full, if the deliverable is not in his hands within 10 business days after the last of three things: his blank sheet, his numbers, his payment.

Proof policy: the only proof is his own document and numbers, plus your own paperwork run on the rule you sell. A blank template watermarked TEMPLATE may be shown. No fictional filled example anywhere. Case-study permission is an opt-in on one page, never a condition, never paid for.

Web rail: one screen, only after a handshake or a referral, so a referrer has somewhere to send him and he can check you are real.

## Stage 4: The Quote Check

Never say "audit" to a buyer. Two versions.

Pocket read, free, 45 minutes, public facts only, stays in your pocket until he asks: Google and Yelp rating and count with the date checked; owner reply rate; quote-request path; name and phone match across Google, Facebook, Yelp; last public post date; the quote sheet scored only from a sheet he hands over. No timed calls, no mystery shopping, no recording. Phone answering is scored from what he tells you.

Paid one page, inside rung 1: five rows ranked by dollars to him. His own numbers outrank any vendor figure. Each row carries what is true today (FACT, dated), compared with whose standard, what it may be costing (YOU SAID, HYPOTHESIS, or UNKNOWN), one next step, cost to fix, who and by when. Formulas on the back. No grades, colors, or adjectives about his work. Vendor figures appear only as "one national vendor's figure." The page carries: "I am not a lawyer, CPA, or insurance adviser. Nothing here is a guarantee. Nothing is published unless you say so in writing."

The two versions cover eight dimensions: quote document, online presence, reviews and response, enquiry response, payment and deposit path, silent-quote follow-up, referral mechanism, and season and capacity (recorded, not scored). Method and scoring in `references/audit-rubric-and-dollar-ranking.md`.

## Stage 5: Sign, intake, onboard

The gate is signature plus payment. Nothing starts before both.

Pre-sale five, on an index card, in this order: the slowest part of getting paid; when it goes quiet; how people reach him and who answers on a ladder; what a typical job runs; how many quotes went out last month and how many said yes. His answers are direct customer evidence and outrank every benchmark in this skill.

Intake, 15 questions, on the delivery visit or one 20-minute call. Layer A reuses the fields in local-service-sales-page's client intake template. Layer B is the nine numbers no published template asks: average job; quotes sent and won last month; where the last five jobs came from; jobs per week he can take; busy and slow months; cash, check, and card mix; days from done to final check; missed calls per week; who signs and who answers the phone. No customer names, no logins, no bank details.

Onboarding, 10 days, one page (`assets/onboarding-10-day.md`): day 0 welcome sheet with four promises (no password, nothing sent in your name, nothing published without your OK, no guarantee and nothing renews); day 0 to 2 intake plus "what would make this a waste of your money?" recorded word for word; day 3 draft; day 5 his one round of changes; day 7 delivery in person with one real quote written together; day 10 one text only if he texts; day 20 or 30 check-in. Delivered means the pad, card, log, and page are in his hands and a walkthrough was held or offered twice. Usage is logged as evidence, not as acceptance.

The homeowner script goes on the back of the pad for the long-time customer who has paid on a handshake for years: "I've started putting everything on paper so nobody has to remember what we agreed. Half when we start, the rest when you've walked it with me and you're happy." Test it on one real quote and a 10-sheet trial pad before a pad of fifty is printed. Name the spouse or bookkeeper in the intake and invite them to the delivery visit; half the paperwork lands on that desk.

## Stage 6: Web presence

Now: one screen, capped at four build hours, published only after the business name is settled and the prelaunch truth gate in local-service-sales-page passes. Header with tap-to-call and tap-to-text. Hero: "One page between your quote and your last check," the first price, the five promises. How it works in three lines. A real photo and the straight-talk line. Where and when. Contact. Footer with the not-a-lawyer, CPA, or insurance-adviser line. No form, no price above the first price, no statistics, no testimonials, no stock photos. Brief and builder prompt in `assets/credibility-page-brief.md`.

Also now, at zero build hours: a Google Business Profile and a Facebook page with identical name and phone.

Later: the full two-page funnel in `assets/full-funnel-page-brief.md`. Build it when both trip: three paid clients on one unchanged offer, and four or more unprompted inbound contacts in any 30 days. Interim rule: if three separate referrers ask for a link, put the one screen live that week.

## Stage 7: Calendar, rhythm, and stop rules

Fixed points for a first-client push: exterior painters are unpitchable in their prime weeks (in the southern Appalachians, roughly mid-September to November 1) except on rain days at a counter or tailgate, never on a job site; the last signature that can still become a reference is the deadline minus 10 delivery days minus a 20-day check-in; late November and December go to deer season and holidays. Detail in `references/season-stop-rules-and-ethics.md`.

Weekly rhythm: at least 70% of business hours go to conversations and follow-ups once selling starts; before that, 50/50. Two early counter mornings a week. A Friday tally (`assets/tally-sheet.md`): conversations, sheets seen, offers, signed, delivered, unprompted inbound, selling hours, building hours, hours per client, cash out.

Stop rules: 10 conversations and zero sheets seen, change the ask, not the product. Five offers and zero signed, change the trade or the scope, not the price. Any week building hours beat selling hours, cut the build. Fewer than 15 names with a venue by the end of week one, the market size is the finding; decide that day on remote clients. Cash out before the first signature over one sale's worth, stop spending.

Build cap before the first signature: three artifacts, the one-pager, the one-page letter, the target list with venues. Every other template is filled the day after the first real blank sheet is seen, from that sheet.

## Stage 8: Check-in, proof, and the second sale

At day 20 or 30: read the quote log together; ask the permission question and record yes or no; make the referral ask with three cards, never at the moment of payment; put the tune-up on his calendar; report back to the referrer within 14 days. A reference means paid in full, delivered on time, permission answered. "Used it on a real quote" is logged as evidence whenever it happens; it is not a condition.

The strategy package (rung 3) is written on the SBA five-part structure (what you sell, to whom, goals, strategies and tactics, metrics) filled with his labeled numbers, and it compares his marketing cost to his revenue inside the plan. It sells only in the off-season, to a client who has bought once and asks.

On the goal date, write the SOP from what actually happened, with every skipped step logged with a reason. That document, not the landing page, is the reusable asset.
