# Offer, Copy, and Page System

Use this reference after the evidence ledger exists.

## Offer card

Create one offer card per audience and page.

| Component | Required answer |
|---|---|
| Audience | Who is this specifically for? |
| Trigger | What happened that made the service relevant now? |
| Job | What progress is the buyer trying to make? |
| Outcome | What changes after successful service? |
| Service | What is being performed? |
| Inclusions | What is always included? |
| Exclusions | What is not included? |
| Add-ons | What can be selected separately? |
| Method | How does the service work? |
| Proof | What verified evidence reduces risk? |
| Price path | Price, starting price, range, walkthrough, or quote logic |
| Risk reduction | Accurate policy, process, credential, or expectation |
| Customer role | What information, preparation, access, or approval is required? |
| Geography | Where is the service truly available? |
| Capacity | What availability can be supported? |
| Action | What should the visitor do? |
| Next state | Request, call, quote, walkthrough, pending appointment, or confirmed booking |

## Offer review

Reject or revise the offer when:

- Scope depends on unstated conditions
- The operation cannot price or schedule it consistently
- The promise exceeds the process
- Proof is generic, borrowed, or invented
- Risk is shifted to the customer without explanation
- The CTA implies confirmation when the business only receives an inquiry
- The economics depend on an unverified discount
- Capacity cannot support the advertised demand

## Booking-readiness test

Answer yes or no:

1. Is the purchasable service defined?
2. Can price be calculated or accurately presented before confirmation?
3. Can the system verify geography?
4. Can it verify current availability?
5. Does it handle payment at the correct time?
6. Does it distinguish requested from confirmed appointments?
7. Are preparation instructions accurate?
8. Are cancellation and rescheduling terms available?
9. Can the team honor the promised scope and timing?
10. Has the full path been tested on mobile and desktop?

Any material “no” blocks a direct booking claim. Use a quote or appointment request.

## Page-system patterns

### Navigable website or homepage

Purpose: establish identity and route intent.

Primary action: the highest-value valid action, usually quote, availability, call, or appointment request.

Secondary behavior: service navigation, lower-intent education, or contact alternative. A lead magnet belongs below the main sales path or on a dedicated page.

### High-intent service page

Purpose: convert visitors who recognize the service need.

Use one segment, one service, one location promise, and one CTA. Match the ad, search query, email, social post, or referral promise that sent the visitor.

### Lead-magnet page

Purpose: exchange one specific useful resource for permission to follow up.

The resource should solve an immediate adjacent problem and naturally lead to the paid service. A short service-selection diagnostic often fits local services better than a broad DIY guide, but this is a hypothesis to validate.

Required path:

1. Dedicated page
2. Minimal form
3. Custom thank-you page
4. Immediate access or accurate delivery expectation
5. Delivery email
6. Short relevant follow-up
7. Low-pressure paid-service next step

Measure downstream qualified leads and booked work, not downloads alone.

### Newsletter page

A newsletter is a product, not a footer label. Define:

- Name
- Audience
- Recurring benefit
- Honest cadence
- Example topics
- Sender identity
- Unsubscribe path

Do not promise a cadence the team cannot sustain. Do not silently subscribe quote leads.

### Thank-you page

Confirm exactly what happened. Then offer one logical next action:

- Download the promised resource
- Expect a reply within a verified window
- Provide missing qualification information
- Schedule a verified next step
- Review preparation guidance

Do not pretend a request is confirmed.

## High-intent page wireframe

1. Simplified header
   - Logo
   - Optional secondary call or text route
   - Minimal campaign navigation
2. Hero
   - Service and geographic qualifier
   - Customer outcome
   - One primary CTA
   - Honest microcopy about the next step
   - Authentic relevant image
3. Trust strip
   - Only verified credentials, review data, locality, or policies
4. Service fit
   - Who and what the service is for
5. Outcomes
   - Benefits in customer language
6. How it works
   - Inquiry or selection
   - Quote or confirmation
   - Service delivery
7. Scope
   - Inclusions, exclusions, add-ons, and conditions
8. Proof
   - Authentic reviews, permitted photos, process evidence, or specific policies
9. Price or quote path
   - Explain how cost and confirmation work
10. Service area and availability
11. Research-backed FAQ
12. Final CTA and form
13. Footer
   - Contact, privacy, terms, and accessibility information

Remove any section without evidence or conversion purpose.

## Hero construction

The hero needs five answers:

1. What is offered?
2. Who or where is it for?
3. What outcome matters?
4. Why should this provider be considered?
5. What happens after the click?

Template:

- Eyebrow: [service or geographic qualifier]
- Headline: [specific meaningful outcome]
- Subheadline: [scope, method, or differentiator supported by evidence]
- CTA: [accurate action]
- Microcopy: [accurate next step or low-risk expectation]
- Proof: [verified signal]

Do not force all five answers into the headline. Use the visual hierarchy.

## Copy sequence

1. Mirror the visitor's source and intent.
2. Name the situation without dramatizing it.
3. Show the desired progress.
4. Explain the offer and its fit.
5. Translate features through “so that” into outcomes.
6. Explain the process and next state.
7. Define scope and price path.
8. Supply relevant proof.
9. Resolve actual objections.
10. Restate the action.

Prefer concrete language over empty adjectives. “We reply with a written quote after reviewing your service details” is useful if true. “Premium solutions tailored to your lifestyle” is not.

## CTA accuracy

Match the label to the actual state:

- Check Availability: capacity is checked after the action
- Request My Quote: the business reviews details and sends a quote
- Request an Appointment: a time is requested but not confirmed
- Schedule a Walkthrough: the visitor chooses or requests a walkthrough
- Book My Service: service, price, availability, and confirmation are handled reliably
- Get the Guide: the resource is immediately available or accurately delivered
- Join [Newsletter Name]: the visitor is subscribing to the described publication

Avoid “Submit,” vague “Get Started,” or pressure-based copy.

## Form design

Ask only what is needed for the immediate decision.

### First-step residential or consumer request

- Service type
- City or postal code
- Name
- Preferred contact method
- Matching email or phone field

Move property size, detailed condition, frequency, photos, add-ons, dates, and notes to a second step or follow-up unless they are essential for triage.

### Commercial or property inquiry

May require:

- Property or business type
- Service location
- Approximate size or scope
- Desired frequency
- Service window
- Contact name and business contact
- Walkthrough request

### Lead magnet or newsletter

- Email as the normal required field
- Name optional unless operationally necessary
- Clear delivery or publication promise
- Privacy link
- Marketing permission separate from service contact

Never collect access codes, keys, alarm information, payment data, or sensitive property details in a marketing form.

## Proof hierarchy

Prefer:

1. Authentic customer statements with permission and context
2. Verified public review source and current count
3. Permitted before-and-after or completed-work photos
4. Verified credentials and policies
5. Specific process evidence
6. Relevant owner or team experience that can be substantiated

Do not create testimonial-style copy when no customer evidence exists. Replace the proof section with process clarity, owner visibility, authentic imagery, and a plan to collect real proof.

## Visual direction

Translate the approved brand into a conversion hierarchy:

- One dominant CTA color
- High-contrast text and controls
- Clear type scale
- Generous whitespace
- Authentic photography near the promise and proof
- Repeated visual cues for scope, process, and next action
- Minimal decorative motion
- Mobile-first reading order

If multiple brand directions exist, present the conflict and require a canonical choice before production styling. Do not mix logo, typography, and palette systems into an accidental third brand.
