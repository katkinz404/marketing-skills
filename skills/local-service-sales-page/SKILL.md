# Local-Service Sales Page

Build the conversion system before polishing the artifact. A page cannot compensate for an unclear service, an untested lead route, missing capacity, or invented proof.

## Non-negotiable rules

- Distinguish verified business facts, direct customer evidence, hypotheses, and unknowns.
- Never invent services, locations, availability, capacity, prices, discounts, guarantees, credentials, review counts, testimonials, outcomes, or operational policies.
- A quote request is not a booking. Make the CTA and confirmation language describe the actual next state.
- Never use fake scarcity, countdowns, unsupported superlatives, or pressure disguised as urgency.
- Do not publish, connect a live domain, spend money, launch an experiment, or send messages without explicit user approval.
- Keep each campaign page focused on one audience, one traffic promise, and one primary action.
- Optimize for qualified leads and booked work, not raw clicks, form fills, or email addresses alone.
- Do not carry one client's facts, language, proof, or conclusions into another client's work.

## Phase 1: Frame the request

State:

- Business and service category
- Geography
- Candidate audience or segments
- Traffic source
- Visitor awareness and intent
- Business objective
- Actual conversion mechanism
- Platform and integrations
- Current evidence
- Explicit constraints and approval boundaries

Translate vague goals such as “capture everyone” into a measurable system. A useful objective is usually to increase qualified completed actions while preserving lead quality, trust, accessibility, and operational fit.

If the user requests one page for unrelated intents, identify whether they need:

- A navigable website or homepage that routes intent
- A high-intent sales or quote page with one primary action
- A service-specific campaign page
- A lead-magnet squeeze page
- A newsletter signup page
- A thank-you or confirmation page

Do not give three equal hero CTAs. Route different intents to purpose-built pages or place the lower-commitment action later in the page.

## Phase 2: Build the truth ledger

Create a ledger with these fields:

| Statement | Evidence class | Source | Date | Confidence | Publication status | Owner or next check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use exactly these evidence classes:

1. Verified business fact
2. Direct customer evidence
3. Hypothesis to test
4. Unknown; do not publish as fact

Mark old facts as dated. A past screenshot or draft is not proof of the current service, capacity, price, or workflow.

Before public copy, verify at minimum:

- Service and scope
- Inclusions, exclusions, and add-ons
- Service area
- Audience fit
- Price or quote method
- Current capacity and lead time if mentioned
- Form, phone, text, calendar, payment, and notification destinations
- Appointment request versus confirmed booking
- Credentials and claims
- Review and photo authenticity and permission
- Cancellation, rescheduling, refund, reclean, access, and privacy implications where relevant

Missing facts do not require abandoning all work. Create a clearly labeled provisional brief, use placeholders, and record the exact decision or evidence needed before publication.

## Phase 3: Select one segment and one job

Do not combine segments merely because the business can serve them. Compare each candidate on:

- Triggering situation
- Desired outcome
- Buying authority
- Decision criteria
- Perceived risk
- Service and proof required
- Buying process
- Economics and capacity fit
- Traffic source and message match

Choose one audience and one triggering job for each campaign page. If evidence is insufficient, label the choice as a test hypothesis and state what would disconfirm it.

## Phase 4: Conduct customer research

Use behavior-first research, not persona invention. Prioritize recent customer and lost-lead interviews, inquiry messages, call notes, form submissions, reviews, complaints, search data, and observed operational records.

Ask for real episodes:

- What happened that made you look for help?
- What were you trying to make possible?
- What alternatives did you consider?
- What felt risky?
- What information did you need before reaching out?
- What nearly stopped you?
- What happened after you contacted a provider?

Avoid leading questions, future hypotheticals, and requests for slogans. Preserve exact language with source and context. Use the synthesis method in references/evidence-and-research.md.

If no direct customer evidence exists, do not present desk research or owner assumptions as voice of customer. Draft provisional messages and an evidence collection plan instead.

## Phase 5: Construct the offer

Create one offer card per page:

- Audience
- Trigger
- Desired outcome
- Service and bounded scope
- Inclusions
- Exclusions
- Optional add-ons
- Method or process
- Verified proof
- Price, starting price, or quote method
- Genuine risk reduction
- Customer responsibilities
- Service area and availability conditions
- Next action and exact next state

Map customer jobs, pains, and gains to the service, pain relievers, and gain creators. Delete any component the operation cannot consistently deliver.

Do not set pricing from competitor pages alone. Require labor, travel, supplies, overhead, rework, payment fees, target margin, and capacity inputs before recommending a public price or discount. When inputs are incomplete, specify the quote logic and data required.

## Phase 6: Apply the booking-readiness gate

Use “Book” only if the system can reliably:

- Define the purchasable service
- Determine and present the price
- Verify service area
- Verify capacity
- Take or correctly defer payment
- Confirm the appointment
- Send accurate preparation, cancellation, and rescheduling information
- Honor the advertised time and scope

Otherwise use language such as “Check Availability,” “Request a Cleaning Quote,” “Request an Appointment,” or another accurate equivalent. State clearly when the request is pending.

## Phase 7: Design the page system

Create an intent map before a wireframe. A common local-service system is:

- Website or homepage: route visitors and establish trust
- High-intent service or quote page: convert ready buyers
- Segment-specific page: match a distinct audience or use case
- Lead-magnet page: earn permission from problem-aware visitors
- Thank-you page: fulfill the promise and offer the next logical action
- Newsletter page: only if the business has a real editorial promise and cadence

For every page define:

- Traffic source and promise
- Audience and job
- One primary conversion
- Secondary route, if necessary
- Offer
- Required proof
- Objections
- Form fields
- Success state
- Follow-up owner and timing
- Analytics events

## Phase 8: Write copy and wireframe

Use the existing copywriting skill after the truth ledger and offer card exist. Use page-cro for hierarchy and friction review, then form-cro for the form. Do not let those skills override the evidence rules here.

The hero should answer:

- What is offered?
- Who or where is it for?
- What meaningful outcome is promised?
- Why should the visitor believe it?
- What happens when they act?

Build the body from the offer and actual objections:

1. Hero and expectation-setting microcopy
2. Verified trust signals
3. Service fit
4. Desired outcomes
5. How it works
6. Inclusions, exclusions, and quote or price path
7. Authentic proof
8. Service area and availability conditions
9. Research-backed FAQ
10. Final CTA and form
11. Contact, privacy, terms, and accessibility links

Use authentic, relevant photography. If none exists, mark photo slots and write a shot list. Do not use synthetic or stock imagery as implied evidence of the client's team, work, customers, or property.

Create at most three materially different hero directions. Label each as verified, evidence-informed, or hypothesis. Recommend one and explain the tradeoff.

## Phase 9: Specify the implementation

For Leadpages, map the selected architecture to the correct surface:

- New site or existing-site page for the navigable website
- Landing page for a campaign with one conversion goal
- Thank-you page for fulfillment and the next step
- Form plus CRM or email integration for capture and follow-up

Create a builder prompt containing:

- Page type, audience, traffic source, and one goal
- Verified business facts
- Offer and exact CTA state
- Section order
- Brand kit and photography rules
- Form fields and consent boundaries
- Mobile, accessibility, and performance requirements
- Claims and content to exclude
- Required integrations, redirect, and analytics

If no approved brand kit exists, instruct the builder to create a neutral wireframe instead of inventing a logo, palette, or brand identity.

## Phase 10: Design the full conversion plumbing

Specify:

- Form storage and owner
- Submission notification
- CRM or email destination
- Segmentation tags
- Thank-you page
- Lead-magnet access and delivery email
- Quote, calendar, or payment handoff
- Request and confirmation messages
- Marketing permission and unsubscribe path
- Error and recovery states
- Mobile call or text links where appropriate

Never ask for door codes, alarm codes, payment details, or sensitive access information in a marketing form. Separate service-request contact from optional marketing permission.

## Phase 11: Measure business outcomes

Use analytics-tracking to create the event plan. At minimum distinguish:

- Page view
- Primary CTA click
- Phone or text click
- Form start
- Form success
- Generate lead
- Appointment request
- Appointment confirmed
- Lead qualified or disqualified
- Booked job
- Completed job
- Revenue or value when reliable
- Lead-magnet or newsletter opt-in and downstream quote

Do not send names, emails, phone numbers, addresses, access notes, or other personal data in URLs, UTMs, or analytics events.

Choose one primary business metric and at least one guardrail. Examples:

- Primary: qualified quote requests or booked jobs
- Guardrails: unqualified lead rate, response delay, cancellation rate, no-show rate, or gross margin

## Phase 12: QA and test

Run the live, published page through the checklist in references/leadpages-build-measurement-and-qa.md. Preview success is not end-to-end proof.

Use ab-test-setup only after recording:

- Evidence-based hypothesis
- Audience and traffic source
- Control and materially different variant
- Primary and guardrail metrics
- Baseline if available
- Minimum detectable effect or practical decision threshold
- Allocation and stopping approach
- Downstream lead-quality review

For low traffic, prefer materially different offers or messages plus customer and usability research over button-color tests. Never declare a winner from a short spike or an arbitrary universal visitor count.

## Required output bundle

Unless the user requests a narrower artifact, deliver:

1. Executive decision and recommended page system
2. Evidence and unknown ledger
3. Customer research plan and synthesis
4. Segment choice with confidence
5. Offer card and booking-readiness result
6. Page map and wireframe
7. Copy deck with claim labels and placeholders
8. Form, follow-up, and consent specification
9. Leadpages implementation map and paste-ready builder prompt when applicable
10. Analytics plan and test backlog
11. Prelaunch go or no-go checklist
12. Next action small enough to complete immediately

## Completion standard

The artifact is complete when the recommended page can be traced to evidence, the offer is operationally deliverable, the CTA describes the true next state, the lead reaches an accountable owner, the visitor receives a correct confirmation, measurement reaches the business outcome, and every unresolved claim is visibly held from publication.
