# Personal-Network Social Campaigns

Use this when the owner of a local service business has long-standing personal profiles on Facebook and Nextdoor, real relationships in the service area, and wants to turn that trust into leads through manual outreach. It defines what each platform allows, five campaigns, message variants built on named psychology levers, a manual workflow with caps, and a low-volume testing method.

This is guidance, not legal or platform-policy advice. Re-verify every platform rule on the live page before launch; platform rules change without notice.

## What the platforms allow (re-verify before launch)

| Platform | Allowed from a personal account | Not allowed from a personal account | Where promotion belongs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nextdoor | Being a neighbor: replies, questions, genuine recommendations of other businesses | Promoting your own business, including posts, comments, and unsolicited direct messages (help.nextdoor.com "Promoting a business or service on Nextdoor"; blog.nextdoor.com self-promotion update; platform_primary, snippet-confirmed 2026-09-15) | Business Page, Business Posts, neighbor Recommendations, Nextdoor Ads |
| Facebook | A real person occasionally sharing their work; warm one-to-one messages to people who know you; replies in groups within each group's rules | A profile used mainly for commercial purposes; cold messages to strangers; repeated identical messages (facebook.com/terms.php and Messenger limits help 177066345680802; platform_primary, snippet-confirmed) | Business Page for offers and ads; personal profile for the human story |

Operating rules that follow from the table:

- **Direct messages go only to people who already know you.** Anyone else meets you through a post, a reply, or a recommendation, and messages you first.
- **On Nextdoor, promotion is Business Page only.** The personal account stays a neighbor account. When a neighbor asks for a recommendation, the compliant answer is other neighbors recommending you and the Business Page replying.
- **Every Facebook group has its own rules.** Read them before the first reply. Many allow business replies only on a set day or only in a pinned thread. Follow the rule or skip the group.
- **No automation, no bulk sends, no identical text.** Vary every message. Cap sends per day.
- **No incentives for reviews or recommendations, anywhere.** Google, Nextdoor, and the FTC rule all prohibit it (see references/compliance-floor.md).
- **Client facts, names, and message logs live in the private workspace, never in this repository.**

## The trust asset and how it is spent

A decades-old personal profile is proof of a real, responsible person. It is spent by being visible where a stranger looks before calling: the profile itself, the comments on a post, the reply in a group thread, the Recommendation count on Nextdoor. It is wasted by sending strangers messages.

Ratio to hold: about four personal or helpful posts for every one business post. The business post works because the other four exist.

## Psychology levers used, and the honesty rule for each

| Lever | How it shows up | Honesty rule |
|---|---|---|
| Liking and similarity | Shared town, shared years, shared references in the message | Only reference real shared history |
| Unity (in-group) | "Neighbors," the town name, local landmarks | Do not claim community ties you do not have |
| Social proof | Real review count, real recommendations, real customer comments | Counts only; never round up; no "hundreds of families" unless true |
| Authority through experience | Years in the trade, specific competence (move-outs, rentals, fine surfaces) | State what is true; never claim licenses or bonding you lack |
| Reciprocity | Free, specific help in posts and replies before any ask | Help must be genuinely useful and not a bait |
| Commitment and consistency | A small first yes (may I send the link?) before the ask | Never manufacture the yes |
| Mere exposure | Regular posting cadence | Never buy reach that looks organic |
| Peak-end | Warm closes and thank-yous | Send them even when the answer is no |

Refuse: fake scarcity, countdowns, "only 3 spots" unless the calendar proves it, invented testimonials, and any statistic from the refuse list.

## The five campaigns

### Campaign 1: Warm reconnect (people you have served)

- **Goal:** reviews first, then rebooking and referrals, in that order and never in the same message.
- **Channel:** Facebook Messenger from the personal profile, one to one.
- **Levers:** liking, peak-end, commitment.
- **Playbook:** run `past-customer-review-outreach.md` first. Two weeks or more after the review conversation closes, send a separate rebooking or referral note. Pace both.
- **Track:** asks, replies, reviews, rebook requests, referrals named.

### Campaign 2: The announcement post

- **Goal:** tell everyone who already trusts you that you are taking clients, and let them share it.
- **Channel:** one post on the personal Facebook profile; the same story as a Business Post on the Nextdoor Business Page; pinned to the Facebook Business Page.
- **Levers:** unity, authority, social proof (comments), reciprocity (ask for shares, not sales).
- **Shape:** a real photo of the owner; the story in first person (years, why now, what you do, where); one clear ask ("if you know someone who needs help, send them my way" plus the contact route); no price, no discount, no urgency.
- **Workflow:** reply to every comment within the hour during the day. Anyone who comments has opened a warm channel; a one-to-one thank-you message to commenters is normal and welcome. Do not message people who only reacted.
- **Cadence:** once; a follow-up "thank you, here is what happened" post two to three weeks later with real results (first reviews, first jobs) if there are any.

### Campaign 3: Recommendation-thread capture

- **Goal:** be the answer when a neighbor asks "anyone know a cleaner?"
- **Channel:** local Facebook groups (personal profile, within group rules); Nextdoor (Business Page reply, plus neighbors' Recommendations).
- **Levers:** social proof, liking, authority.
- **Two moves:**
  1. **Advocates.** Ask five to ten past customers, individually and without any incentive, whether they would be willing to mention you when they see someone asking. Give them the Business Page link so a tag works. This is the single highest-leverage ask in this playbook.
  2. **Owner replies.** When you reply yourself, lead with the useful answer to the question (availability, what you do, how to reach you), one or two sentences, no pitch, and invite a direct message. Never reply to the same thread twice unless asked.
- **Caps:** at most five group replies per day; skip any group whose rules prohibit it; never post the same text in two groups.

### Campaign 4: Nextdoor Recommendations drive

- **Goal:** turn the Business Page into a recommended business with a real count.
- **Channel:** Nextdoor Business Page (set up, verified, categories, service area, hours, photos); Business Posts twice a month.
- **Levers:** social proof, unity.
- **Workflow:** ask past customers who are on Nextdoor, one to one and without incentive, to recommend the page. Pace like reviews (four to five asks a week). Reply to every recommendation from the Business Page.
- **Do not:** post promotions from the personal account; message neighbors you do not know; use Opportunity Alerts or Ads until the intake gate has passed.

### Campaign 5: Partners and property owners

- **Goal:** recurring work from people who control many homes: realtors, property managers, short-term-rental hosts, second-home owners.
- **Channel:** one-to-one messages only to people you already know; for strangers, professional channels (their business email or office phone), not social DMs.
- **Levers:** authority, reciprocity (a specific, useful offer such as a turnover checklist), commitment (a small first job).
- **Offer:** a flat referral fee per booked job under a written agreement, or a standing turnover arrangement; never tied to reviews.
- **Track:** conversations, agreements signed, jobs per partner, margin per job (turnovers can be low-margin; check against `cpl-ceiling-model.md`).

### Content backbone (supports every campaign)

Two to three posts per week across the personal profile and the Business Page, in this mix: a real moment from life or work, a genuinely useful tip for the area (seasonal, local housing types, rental turnover), proof of work with written permission (before and after, no addresses, no faces without consent), and the occasional business post. Post the business content to the Business Page and share it to the personal profile, rather than the reverse.

## Manual workflow

| When | Action | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| Daily, morning | Check Messenger, group threads, Nextdoor page inbox and Recommendations; reply to everything within the hour during answered hours | |
| Daily | Warm DMs from the tracker (Campaign 1, 4, 5) | 5 per day on Facebook, 2 per day on Nextdoor, none to strangers |
| Daily | Group and thread replies (Campaign 3) | 5 per day, each group's rules first |
| Twice weekly | Content post | 2-3 per week, 4:1 personal to business |
| Weekly, Monday | Scorecard rows below; rotate message variants; decide pauses | |
| Weekly, Thursday | Owner approves next week's variants and any new group or partner | |

Stop signals: a Messenger sending warning, a group admin warning or removal, a Nextdoor content flag, a review or recommendation that vanishes after 72 hours, or the next two weeks more than 90% scheduled. Any of these pauses new outreach for at least 24 hours (capacity: until slots open).

## Testing at low volume

A/B testing needs volume this channel does not have. Use variant rotation and counts:

1. Write three variants per message type (A, B, C), each built on a different lead lever.
2. Rotate strictly: A, B, C, A, B, C, logged per send.
3. Decision rule: after each variant has 15 sends, compare reply counts and booked-job counts. Keep the leader and the runner-up, retire the third, write a new C that changes one thing (opener, ask, or close).
4. Judge on replies and booked jobs, never on likes or reactions.
5. Review weekly. Below 15 sends per variant, report counts and make no decision.

## Scorecard rows (add to the weekly scorecard)

- Warm DMs sent by campaign and variant; replies; booked-job requests
- Group and thread replies made; inbound messages that followed
- Advocates recruited; times you were tagged or mentioned
- Nextdoor Recommendations count and change
- Announcement post: comments, shares, inbound messages, jobs traced to it
- Partner conversations, agreements, jobs
- Stop signals hit this week

## Message shapes

Every message is written for one person, references one real shared thing, asks one thing, and closes warm. The shapes below are skeletons; the private workspace holds the real variants with real details.

**Warm rebooking (Campaign 1, two or more weeks after the review thread closes)**

- A (liking): "Hi [name], I keep thinking about [real detail]. I'm taking regular clients again in [area]. If a [weekly / every-other-week / monthly] clean would make life easier, I'd love to set something up. If not, no worries at all."
- B (reciprocity): lead with one specific, useful tip for their situation, then the same one-sentence offer.
- C (commitment): "Quick question first: is your place still [detail]? If so I have an idea that might help."

**Announcement post (Campaign 2)**

- The story (three to five sentences, first person), the ask ("if you know someone who could use a hand, send them my way"), the route (page link or number). Photo of the owner.

**Group reply (Campaign 3)**

- "I do this in [area] and have for [years]. Happy to help if it fits; message me and I'll tell you what I can do this month." Nothing more.

**Advocate ask (Campaign 3)**

- "Can I ask a small favor? If you ever see someone in [group or Nextdoor] asking for a cleaner and you'd feel comfortable mentioning me, that would mean a lot. Here's my page so a tag works: [link]. And it's completely fine to say no."

**Recommendation ask (Campaign 4)**

- "I set up a Nextdoor page for the business and it has no recommendations yet. If you'd be willing to recommend it, here's the link: [link]. Only if it's true for you, and no hard feelings if not."

**Partner note (Campaign 5)**

- "Hi [name], I know you [manage / list / host] a few places in [area]. I've done [type of work] for [years] and I'm setting up regular turnover and move-out cleans. If it would help to have someone reliable on call, I'd like to talk about how you'd want that to work." Then, if they engage, the written flat-fee terms.
